## 1. Formulation Principles & Statistical Standards

In strict accordance with academic standards and Phase 03 directives:
1. **Empirical Justification**: Hypotheses are formulated only where quantitative or empirical testing is feasible and justified by verified literature.
2. **Explicit Variable Formulation**: Every hypothesis formally specifies independent variables, dependent variables, directional comparisons, quantitative evaluation metrics, and validated statistical testing protocols.
3. **Conservative Methodological Restraint**: No statistical tests are fabricated or prematurely assumed; each test is selected based on established conventions documented in the primary research corpus.

### Hypothesis 1 (H1): Spatial Layout Document Intelligence in ATS Screening
- **Hypothesis Identifier**: `H1` (Maps to `RQ1`, `RO1`, `CG4`)
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_{0,1}$)**: There is no statistically significant difference in Named Entity Recognition F1-scores on multi-column technical resumes between a spatial vision-language model (LayoutLMv3) and standard linear text extraction pipelines ($\mu_{\text{LayoutLMv3}} \le \mu_{\text{Linear}}$).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_{1,1}$)**: A spatial vision-language model (LayoutLMv3) achieves statistically significantly higher Named Entity Recognition F1-scores on multi-column technical resumes than standard linear text extraction pipelines ($\mu_{\text{LayoutLMv3}} > \mu_{\text{Linear}}$).
- **Variables**:
  - *Independent Variable*: Document parsing architecture (LayoutLMv3 spatial 2D bounding boxes vs. linear text extraction via PyPDF2 / pdfminer).
  - *Dependent Variable*: Named Entity Recognition Macro-F1 score across four target entities (`Education`, `Experience`, `Technical Skills`, `Certifications`).
  - *Controlled Variables*: Resume sample size (N=2,500), resume visual layouts (50% single-column, 50% two-column/multi-column), ground-truth annotation rubrics.
- **Expected Comparison**: LayoutLMv3 will eliminate horizontal text interleaving across columns, producing a statistically significant F1 gain of at least $+15\%$ on multi-column formats (matching Kapula P42's 94.8% Entity F1 vs 66.4% linear baseline).
- **Evaluation Metric**: Macro-averaged Entity Extraction F1-Score; Layout boundary tolerance percentage.
- **Statistical Testing Protocol**: Paired two-sample $t$-test (or Wilcoxon signed-rank test if normality assumption is violated via Shapiro-Wilk test) across 10-fold cross-validation splits, evaluated at significance level $\alpha = 0.01$.

### Hypothesis 2 (H2): Multimodal Conversational Latency & Human Panel Correlation
- **Hypothesis Identifier**: `H2` (Maps to `RQ2`, `RO2`, `CG5`)
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_{0,2}$)**: The automated technical and behavioral scores generated by the PRIE WebRTC multimodal interview engine do not exhibit a statistically significant positive correlation ($\rho \le 0.70$) with independent human corporate recruiter panel evaluations.
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_{1,2}$)**: The automated technical and behavioral scores generated by the PRIE WebRTC multimodal interview engine exhibit a statistically significant, strong positive correlation ($\rho \ge 0.82$) with independent human corporate recruiter panel evaluations.
- **Variables**:
  - *Independent Variable*: Candidate interview performance evaluated across real-time acoustic prosody (openSMILE), facial landmarks (MediaPipe), and containerized code execution (Docker).
  - *Dependent Variable*: Composite interview readiness score assigned by PRIE vs. composite rubric score assigned by a 5-member corporate recruiter panel.
  - *Controlled Variables*: Conversational turn latency maintained strictly $\le 1.5$ seconds; identical question sets across 100 candidate sessions.
- **Expected Comparison**: The multimodal scoring pipeline will achieve correlation levels meeting or exceeding established literature benchmarks (Pearson $r \ge 0.82$ from Inamdar P15, $r \ge 0.84$ from Vachkal P28, and $r = 0.87$ from Wahid P29).
- **Evaluation Metric**: Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient ($r$) for parametric distributions; Spearman's rank correlation coefficient ($\rho$) for non-parametric ordinal ranks; End-to-end turn latency (milliseconds).
- **Statistical Testing Protocol**: Student's $t$-test for correlation significance against the null threshold $\rho_0 = 0.70$, with two-tailed test evaluated at $\alpha = 0.01$.

### Hypothesis 3 (H3): Longitudinal Multi-Horizon Forecasting vs Static Modeling
- **Hypothesis Identifier**: `H3` (Maps to `RQ3`, `RO3`, `CG3`)
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_{0,3}$)**: A longitudinal sequence model (Temporal Fusion Transformer) trained on weekly multi-variate student preparation activity does not achieve a statistically significant improvement in Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC) for placement readiness compared to static gradient boosted classifiers trained on cumulative academic records ($\text{PR-AUC}_{\text{TFT}} \le \text{PR-AUC}_{\text{CatBoost}}$).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_{1,3}$)**: A longitudinal sequence model (Temporal Fusion Transformer) trained on weekly multi-variate student preparation activity achieves a statistically significant improvement in Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC) for placement readiness compared to static gradient boosted classifiers trained on cumulative academic records ($\text{PR-AUC}_{\text{TFT}} > \text{PR-AUC}_{\text{CatBoost}}$).
- **Variables**:
  - *Independent Variable*: Temporal resolution and modeling architecture (Multi-horizon Temporal Fusion Transformer on weekly clickstream/coding/interview velocity vs. CatBoost / Random Forest on static cumulative GPA and test marks).
  - *Dependent Variable*: Placement readiness classification performance (PR-AUC, Macro-F1, ROC-AUC).
  - *Controlled Variables*: Institutional student cohort size (N=3,000+), identical 80/20 train-test split, identical final placement offer ground truth.
- **Expected Comparison**: The Temporal Fusion Transformer will capture mid-semester preparation velocity and detect at-risk divergence trajectories by Week 4 ($p < 0.001$), yielding a PR-AUC uplift of $\ge +0.08$ over static CatBoost (replicating Azeez P44's 94.6% benchmark).
- **Evaluation Metric**: Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC); Macro-averaged F1-Score; Week-4 Risk Detection Sensitivity.
- **Statistical Testing Protocol**: DeLong's test for comparing paired receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves; McNemar's test on classification contingency tables evaluated at $\alpha = 0.01$.

### Hypothesis 4 (H4): Actionability and Usability of Prescriptive Counterfactuals
- **Hypothesis Identifier**: `H4` (Maps to `RQ4`, `RO4`, `CG2`)
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_{0,4}$)**: Distance-constrained counterfactual explanations (DiCE) over intervenable student variables do not result in a statistically significant increase in actionable task completion rates among at-risk students compared to descriptive game-theoretic feature attributions (TreeSHAP) ($\mu_{\text{DiCE}} \le \mu_{\text{SHAP}}$).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_{1,4}$)**: Distance-constrained counterfactual explanations (DiCE) over intervenable student variables result in a statistically significant increase in actionable task completion rates among at-risk students compared to descriptive game-theoretic feature attributions (TreeSHAP) ($\mu_{\text{DiCE}} > \mu_{\text{SHAP}}$).
- **Variables**:
  - *Independent Variable*: Form of explainable AI presentation (Prescriptive, distance-constrained DiCE counterfactual cards specifying exact intervenable study/practice targets vs. Descriptive TreeSHAP waterfall/summary attribution plots).
  - *Dependent Variable*: Proportion of recommended remedial preparation tasks completed by students within a 4-week trial window; System Usability Scale (SUS) comprehension rating.
  - *Controlled Variables*: Total recommended effort hours (bounded $\le 15$ hrs/week), initial predicted risk category, student academic department.
- **Expected Comparison**: Students receiving actionable counterfactual recourses will demonstrate significantly higher task adherence ($\ge 75\%$ vs $<40\%$ for SHAP) because counterfactuals specify achievable, forward-looking goals rather than highlighting immutable past deficits (extending Kumar P19).
- **Evaluation Metric**: Actionable Task Completion Percentage; System Usability Scale (SUS score out of 100); Counterfactual Validity ($\ge 92\%$).
- **Statistical Testing Protocol**: Two-proportion $z$-test on task completion rates; Independent two-sample Mann-Whitney $U$ test on Likert usability ratings evaluated at $\alpha = 0.01$.

### Hypothesis 5 (H5): Psychometric Discrimination in Causal Concept AQG
- **Hypothesis Identifier**: `H5` (Maps to `RQ5`, `RO5`, `CG7`)
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_{0,5}$)**: Automated technical assessment items generated via Causal Concept DAGs and Bloom's cognitive taxonomy prompting do not achieve statistically significantly higher psychometric Item Discrimination indices ($D$) than items generated via unconstrained zero-shot LLM prompts ($\mu_{D,\text{Causal}} \le \mu_{D,\text{ZeroShot}}$).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_{1,5}$)**: Automated technical assessment items generated via Causal Concept DAGs and Bloom's cognitive taxonomy prompting achieve statistically significantly higher psychometric Item Discrimination indices ($D \ge 0.80$) than items generated via unconstrained zero-shot LLM prompts ($\mu_{D,\text{Causal}} > \mu_{D,\text{ZeroShot}}$).
- **Variables**:
  - *Independent Variable*: Question generation pipeline (Causal Concept DAG + Bloom's Level 3–5 prompting with dual-agent distractor validation vs. Unconstrained zero-shot prompting of foundation LLMs).
  - *Dependent Variable*: Psychometric Item Discrimination Index ($D = P_{\text{upper}} - P_{\text{lower}}$) computed across candidate testing cohorts; Distractor ambiguity error rate.
  - *Controlled Variables*: Technical knowledge domain (Data Structures & Algorithms, Database Systems, Computer Networks), total assessment items (N=1,000), test duration.
- **Expected Comparison**: Causal concept grounding will eliminate distractor ambiguity ($\le 4\%$) and effectively discriminate between high-performing and low-performing candidates ($D \ge 0.80$, matching Wang P25's 0.88 benchmark).
- **Evaluation Metric**: Item Discrimination Index ($D$); Item Difficulty ($p$); Subject Matter Expert (SME) pedagogical validity rating ($\ge 90\%$).
- **Statistical Testing Protocol**: Independent two-sample $t$-test on item discrimination values; Fisher's exact test on SME distractor acceptability rates evaluated at $\alpha = 0.01$.

### Hypothesis 6 (H6): Placement Conversion Uplift in Closed-Loop Digital Twin
- **Hypothesis Identifier**: `H6` (Maps to `RQ6`, `RO6`, `CG1`, `CG6`, `CG8`)
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_{0,6}$)**: Students preparing through the closed-loop PRIE Triangular Placement Digital Twin do not exhibit a statistically significant increase in campus placement offer conversion rates compared to a matched control cohort preparing via unassisted or fragmented traditional tools ($P_{\text{PRIE}} \le P_{\text{Control}}$).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_{1,6}$)**: Students preparing through the closed-loop PRIE Triangular Placement Digital Twin exhibit a statistically significant, higher campus placement offer conversion rate compared to a matched control cohort preparing via unassisted or fragmented traditional tools ($P_{\text{PRIE}} > P_{\text{Control}}$).
- **Variables**:
  - *Independent Variable*: Preparation environment (Integrated PRIE Triangular Digital Twin synchronizing ATS, mock interviews, adaptive AQG, and prescriptive counterfactuals vs. Fragmented ad-hoc preparation / traditional campus placement cell training).
  - *Dependent Variable*: Official institutional campus placement offer rate (Binary Placed / Unplaced at end of academic cycle); Time-to-offer duration (weeks).
  - *Controlled Variables*: Academic engineering branch, historical baseline CGPA, gender, institutional tier.
- **Expected Comparison**: Closed-loop continuous feedback and prescriptive remediation will elevate final placement conversion by $\ge +15\%$ (replicating Babureddy P41's $+18.5\%$ conversion gain and Senthil P16's $-18\%$ delay reduction).
- **Evaluation Metric**: Final Campus Placement Conversion Percentage; Time-to-First-Offer (days); Institutional Digital Twin Fidelity ($\ge 90\%$).
- **Statistical Testing Protocol**: Two-sample test of proportions ($z$-test); Log-rank test and Cox proportional hazards regression for time-to-placement survival curves, evaluated at $\alpha = 0.01$.
