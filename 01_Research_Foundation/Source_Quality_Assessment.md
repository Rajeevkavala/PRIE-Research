# Source Quality Assessment — Phase 01 Research Foundation Rebuild

This document provides a systematic, evidence-grounded quality appraisal of the **44 downloaded research papers** and **4 un-downloaded references** constituting the Phase 01 research foundation for **ScholarCamp** and its core engine **PRIE (Placement Readiness Intelligence Engine)**.

In strict compliance with academic standards and Phase 01 directives, **no arbitrary numerical quality scores** are assigned. Instead, each study is qualitatively evaluated across seven objective transparency and rigor dimensions:
1. **Venue & Indexing**: Type of venue (Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4 journal, IEEE/ACM conference, regional journal, or preprint).
2. **Publisher**: Academic publishing house or institutional issuing body.
3. **DOI / Persistent Identifier**: Official registration and resolve status.
4. **Peer-Review Status**: Rigor of peer-review screening (e.g., blind peer-reviewed journal/conference vs. preprint).
5. **Dataset Transparency**: Public availability, sample size disclosure, sampling methodology, and ethical privacy handling.
6. **Methodology Transparency**: Detailed architectural diagrams, explicit algorithmic formulations, and mathematical clarity.
7. **Reproducibility & Experimental Transparency**: Availability of open source code/repositories, hyperparameter disclosure, baseline descriptions, and evaluation metric clarity.

## 3. Systematic Findings on Corpus Evidence Quality

Across the 44 primary PDF research papers examined directly, several critical quality patterns emerge:

1. **High Methodological Transparency in Top-Tier Literature**:
   - Papers published in recognized IEEE, ACM, Springer, and MDPI venues (e.g., **Paper 02** [MDPI Higher Education], **Paper 09** [IEEE HNICEM], **Paper 25** [IEEE MIPR], **Paper 26** [IEEE Access], **Paper 27** [ACM Conference], **Paper 31** [IEEE Access], **Paper 32** [Springer EAIT], **Paper 33** [IEEE Access], **Paper 34** [Springer DASC], **Paper 35** [ACM CIKM], **Paper 39** [Springer IJAIED]) exhibit rigorous experimental designs, formal mathematical formulations, comprehensive ablation baselines, and transparent evaluation metrics.

2. **Presence of Regional / Emerging Journal Artifacts**:
   - Several papers published in emerging open-access regional or engineering journals (e.g., **Paper 14**, **Paper 28**, **Paper 30**, **Paper 37**, **Paper 38**) document functional software implementations and UI architectures, but frequently omit statistical benchmarking, public code repositories, or held-out validation splits.
   - **Critical Anomaly in Paper 38**: Paper 38 (*PrepWise*, IJEEE 2026) was identified during inspection as containing unedited boilerplate text from the IEEE conference LaTeX template in its Conclusion section and dummy references from 1989–2002. It is classified strictly as **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** as an architectural design case study rather than an empirical algorithmic benchmark.

3. **Prevalence of Institutional and Private Cohorts**:
   - Over 65% of predictive and employability studies (e.g., Paper 01, 04, 07, 09, 10, 18, 19, 22, 24, 41, 44) rely on private, single-institution datasets collected from student portals or regional survey instruments. While providing authentic educational contexts, this pattern underscores the global lack of standardized public benchmark datasets in higher education employability.

4. **Public Benchmarks in Open Scholarly & NLP Work**:
   - In contrast, literature search and document discovery papers (Paper 13, 16, 20, 39, 43) leverage public open-access APIs (Europe PMC, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Kaggle, SQuAD), providing high replicability.
